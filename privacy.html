DigUp Privacy Policy

Effective date: 2026-11-13

DigUp does not collect, transmit, sell or share any personal or browsing data. Everything happens on your device.

What DigUp does

DigUp is a Chrome extension that reads tables and lists from the web page you are viewing, or from files you choose on your computer, and lets you export them as CSV, Excel, JSON or PDF files, or copy them for Google Sheets.

Data we collect

None. DigUp has no servers, no accounts and no analytics. It does not collect personal information, browsing history, page content or usage statistics, and it sends nothing to us. The only requests it makes are the ones you start or turn on yourself: the detail-page fetches described below, the pages of a list of web addresses you paste, page checks, sending rows to a webhook or a Google Sheet you choose, the text-recognition language data downloaded once if you choose "Read with OCR", requests to Anthropic only if you add your own Claude API key, and, only if you turn on the MCP link under AI tools, a connection to a program on your own computer.

How your data is handled

  • Scraping happens locally. The page is read and processed inside your browser. Nothing is sent to us or to any third party.
  • Saved site setups (the list you chose, column names and the Next button for a site, plus a short fingerprint of the page you saved it on, never the page address itself, so DigUp can tell you when that page's layout may have changed) are stored in chrome.storage.local on your device so DigUp can offer them on your next visit. You can reset them in the panel, or remove them by uninstalling the extension.
  • Recent results are kept in chrome.storage.session so you can restore them if the panel is reopened, and to show them in a tab of DigUp's own when you choose "Open as page". Chrome clears this when the browser closes.
  • Exports are files you download. CSV, Excel, JSON and PDF files are saved to your computer by your browser, and "Copy" places text on your clipboard. What you do with them afterwards is up to you.
  • Edits on the results page. If you edit the table in "Open as page", your edited table is kept in chrome.storage.session while that tab is open, so a reload keeps your changes. "Revert" removes it, and Chrome clears it when the browser closes.

Files on your computer

In the panel's Files mode you can choose files or a folder, or drop them on the panel: saved web pages, CSV, Excel, JSON, Word and PDF files, and pictures of text. DigUp reads them inside your browser, on your computer. They are never uploaded, to us or to anyone else. A saved web page is shown to DigUp without its scripts, and without the images, fonts and styles it points to on the internet: nothing it refers to is loaded. Chrome may use the word "upload" when you choose a folder; the files still stay on your computer.

  • Read again. If you choose a folder, Chrome lets DigUp keep a handle to that folder (never the files or what is in them) in your browser's storage (IndexedDB), so you can read it again later. Chrome asks you before DigUp reads the folder again. DigUp also keeps the setup you used for that folder (the list you chose and its columns) in chrome.storage.local.

Text recognition (OCR)

Scans and photos of text are read with text recognition that runs on your computer (Tesseract.js, which comes with DigUp). It needs language data (about 3 MB for one language). The first time you press "Read with OCR" for a language, DigUp downloads that data from the jsDelivr network (cdn.jsdelivr.net) and your browser keeps it for next time. Like any web request, jsDelivr sees your IP address and which language file is asked for; your files and their text are never sent. DigUp says this in the panel before you press the button.

Details from each page

If you press "Get details" in the panel, DigUp fetches the pages linked from the rows you scraped, only from the same site you are scraping, to read fields such as title, description, price, email and phone. This only happens when you start it, you can stop it at any time, and the results stay on your device. Those pages are requested from that site directly, with your existing sign-in for that site, just as your browser would request them; nothing is sent to us. If a page redirects to another site, the browser follows the redirect as usual and DigUp discards that result.

A list of web addresses

In Files mode you can paste a list of web addresses. When you press "Read these pages", Chrome first asks you to allow DigUp to open those sites. DigUp then requests each page from its site directly, one at a time with a pause between pages, with your existing sign-in for that site, just as your browser would, and reads it on your computer like a saved page. The addresses and pages are not stored after you close the panel, except the setup you used, which is kept in chrome.storage.local per site. Nothing is sent to us. A page that asks you to sign in or to check that you're human is skipped and listed with the reason; DigUp never works around it.

Optional site access

DigUp does not ask for access to all sites when you install it. Chrome asks you to allow access to a site only when you start something that needs it: a multi-page run that uses a Next button (infinite-scroll runs stay on the current page and do not need it), or "Details from each page". You can also choose "Allow DigUp on all sites" in the panel's menu: with it on, DigUp also rescans a tab's page when that page reloads in a tab where DigUp's panel is open. That option is off by default, is granted only by you, and you can switch it off from the same menu or revoke any access at any time in Chrome's extension settings.

Describe columns

When you type the columns you want and press "Get columns", DigUp sends the column names you typed and a few sample values from the first rows to Chrome's built-in AI, which runs on your own computer, to choose the matching fields. Nothing is sent to us or to anyone else. DigUp uses the built-in AI only when its model is already on your computer; it never starts Chrome's model download (about 2–4 GB) unless you press "Use built-in AI". Where the built-in AI is not available, DigUp matches by name without any AI.

If you ask for an AI column (for example "summary of description"), DigUp sends each row's value of that field to the same built-in AI on your computer, one row at a time, and shows its answer in a column marked "(AI)". Nothing is sent to us or anyone else.

Your own Claude API key (optional, off by default)

On DigUp's AI tools page you can add your own API key from Anthropic, so that Describe and AI columns use Claude instead of Chrome's built-in AI. Only then, and only when you use Describe or an AI column, DigUp sends Anthropic's API (api.anthropic.com) the words you typed, the column names and a few sample values (Describe), or the text of each cell an AI column reads. Anthropic handles that data under its own terms and privacy policy, for your account, which it bills. Your key is kept in chrome.storage.local in this browser (never synced) and is sent only to Anthropic. DigUp sends one short test question when you save the key. "Remove key" deletes it, and DigUp goes back to the built-in AI.

AI tools (optional, off by default)

DigUp's AI tools page (DigUp menu, AI tools) has two options. Both are off until you turn them on, both need "Allow DigUp on all sites", and you can turn them off at any time on the same page.

  • Connect AI tools on this computer (MCP). DigUp connects to the DigUp MCP server, a small program you download from that page and run on your own computer, at 127.0.0.1 (your computer only, never the internet). It answers nothing until that program sends the pairing code shown on the page. AI apps you use (such as Claude Code or Cursor) can then ask DigUp, through that program, to list your open web pages, find lists on a page, or scrape a page, which DigUp reads in your own Chrome. What happens to the results is up to that app and its own policies; DigUp sends nothing to us. While the option is on, DigUp checks for the program every 30 seconds to a minute (Chrome's alarms feature) and when you switch back to Chrome, and keeps the option, the port and the pairing code in chrome.storage.local.
  • Let AI agents use DigUp on pages. DigUp adds a small script to the web pages you open, so that a script on a page (for example a browser agent's) can ask DigUp to find or scrape the lists on that same page. It returns only data from that page, which the page can already read, never data from another site. While this is on, websites can see that DigUp is installed.

Both keep at least one second between pages and at most 50 pages, and report bot checks and sign-in pages instead of getting past them.

Sharing setups

"Share" copies a site's setup (which list, its column names and where DigUp reads them, the Next button, the page limit and delay) as text you can send to someone, who imports it on DigUp's Saved setups page. A shared setup never includes the fingerprint of the page you saved it on, page checks, or any scraped data. Importing a setup only saves it in your browser.

Sending rows (optional)

  • Webhook. If you paste a webhook address (for example from an automation tool) and press Send, DigUp posts the rows to that address, and Chrome first asks you to allow DigUp to contact that one site. DigUp remembers the address in chrome.storage.local for next time. What happens to the rows there is up to that service and its own policies.
  • Google Sheets. Where offered, "New Google Sheet" asks you to sign in with Google and to allow DigUp to create files in your Google Drive. DigUp uses Google's narrow drive.file permission, so it can see only the sheets it creates. The rows go from your browser straight to your own Google account; nothing passes through us.

Page checks (optional)

If you turn on "Check this page for changes" for a page, DigUp keeps that page's address and setup in chrome.storage.local, and Chrome first asks you to allow DigUp to open that site by itself. While Chrome is open, every 12 hours, day or week as you chose, DigUp opens the page in a background tab, reads the list, closes the tab, and compares the rows with the last check (Chrome's alarms feature starts each check). The latest rows and what changed stay on your device, shown on DigUp's Saved setups page and as a number on DigUp's icon. If you chose a webhook or a Google Sheet for the changes, they are sent there too. Checks keep at least one second between pages and at most 50 pages, and a bot check or sign-in page stops the check instead of being bypassed. Turning a check off deletes its saved rows.

Desktop alerts. If you leave "Show a desktop alert when something changes" on (or turn Alerts on for a check on the Saved setups page), Chrome first asks you to allow DigUp to show notifications. DigUp then shows a notification on this computer when a check finds changes, or once when a check stops working; it shows the page's name and the first change. Notifications are made by Chrome on your device; nothing is sent anywhere.

First-run tour

The first time you open the panel, DigUp shows a short tour. DigUp records in chrome.storage.local that you finished or skipped it, so it isn't shown again. "Try with sample files" opens three made-up sales files that come with DigUp.

Rating reminder

After a few exports DigUp may show an occasional "Rate DigUp" reminder card, at most once a day; choosing "Don't ask again" stops it. To decide when to show it, DigUp counts how many times you export and records when the card was last shown. These are kept in chrome.storage.local on your device and are never sent anywhere. The card links to the Chrome Web Store only if you click it.

When you remove DigUp

When you remove DigUp, Chrome opens a short page on DigUp's website with quick fixes for common problems and a link to the feedback form. Its address carries only DigUp's version number (for example ?v=1.4.0), nothing about you or the pages you used. The feedback form is optional.

Welcome page

When you first install DigUp it opens a welcome page that is packaged inside the extension. It collects nothing, and its links open only if you click them.

What DigUp does not do

  • No analytics or tracking
  • No advertising
  • No remote code: all code ships inside the extension (OCR language data is data, not code)
  • No selling or sharing of data, because none is collected

Using DigUp responsibly

Please respect each website's terms of use when you collect data from it.
