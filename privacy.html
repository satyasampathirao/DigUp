DigUp Privacy Policy

Effective date: 2026-11-05

DigUp does not collect, transmit, sell or share any personal or browsing data. Everything happens on your device.

What DigUp does

DigUp is a Chrome extension that reads tables and lists from the web page you are viewing and lets you export them as CSV, Excel or JSON files, or copy them for Google Sheets.

Data we collect

None. DigUp has no servers, no accounts and no analytics. It does not collect personal information, browsing history, page content or usage statistics, and it sends nothing to us. The only requests it makes are the detail-page fetches described below, which you start yourself.

How your data is handled

  • Scraping happens locally. The page is read and processed inside your browser. Nothing is sent to us or to any third party.
  • Saved site setups (the list you chose, column names and the Next button for a site, plus a short fingerprint of the page you saved it on, never the page address itself, so DigUp can tell you when that page's layout may have changed) are stored in chrome.storage.local on your device so DigUp can offer them on your next visit. You can reset them in the panel, or remove them by uninstalling the extension.
  • Recent results are kept in chrome.storage.session so you can restore them if the panel is reopened. Chrome clears this when the browser closes.
  • Exports are files you download. CSV, Excel and JSON files are saved to your computer by your browser, and "Copy" places text on your clipboard. What you do with them afterwards is up to you.

Details from each page

If you press "Get details" in the panel, DigUp fetches the pages linked from the rows you scraped, only from the same site you are scraping, to read fields such as title, description, price, email and phone. This only happens when you start it, you can stop it at any time, and the results stay on your device. Those pages are requested from that site directly, with your existing sign-in for that site, just as your browser would request them; nothing is sent to us. If a page redirects to another site, the browser follows the redirect as usual and DigUp discards that result.

Optional site access

DigUp does not ask for access to all sites when you install it. Chrome asks you to allow access to a site only when you start something that needs it: a multi-page run that uses a Next button (infinite-scroll runs stay on the current page and do not need it), or "Details from each page". You can also choose "Allow DigUp on all sites" in the panel's menu: with it on, DigUp also rescans a tab's page when that page reloads in a tab where DigUp's panel is open. That option is off by default, is granted only by you, and you can switch it off from the same menu or revoke any access at any time in Chrome's extension settings.

Rating reminder

After a few exports DigUp may show an occasional "Rate DigUp" reminder card, at most once a day; choosing "Don't ask again" stops it. To decide when to show it, DigUp counts how many times you export and records when the card was last shown. These are kept in chrome.storage.local on your device and are never sent anywhere. The card links to the Chrome Web Store only if you click it.

Welcome page

When you first install DigUp it opens a welcome page that is packaged inside the extension. It collects nothing, and its links open only if you click them.

What DigUp does not do

  • No analytics or tracking
  • No advertising
  • No remote code: all code ships inside the extension
  • No selling or sharing of data, because none is collected

Using DigUp responsibly

Please respect each website's terms of use when you collect data from it.
